## Psychotherapy for trauma

I have been providing trauma treatment for over 25 years. In my experience, trauma, stress and the pressures of modern society are key contributors to both mental disorders and physical illness. Much of what is considered normal in modern culture, especially in health and social interaction, is in fact deeply harmful.

My approach to trauma therapy is built on the belief that much modern illness stems from unresolved trauma, emotional suppression and disconnection from our authentic selves. The key elements of treatment are set out below.

## Authenticity and emotional expression

A keystone of trauma therapy is reclaiming authenticity and emotional honesty as vital for healing. People who suppress emotions, particularly anger, are at greater risk of developing chronic illnesses such as autoimmune disorders. Trauma therapy encourages emotional expression rather than repression, because unprocessed emotions can lead to many mental health ailments as well as inflammation and physical disease.

## Self-compassion and compassion for others

Trauma therapy encourages the practice of deep self-compassion: recognising one's vulnerabilities and traumas without judgement. Acknowledging pain, imperfections and faults is where the healing process begins. Compassionate inquiry, as a therapeutic technique, explores the emotional roots of addiction or illness by asking questions such as "What role did this behaviour or substance play in your life?" This helps clients confront and understand their deeper needs.

## Group therapy and social healing

Healing from trauma is greatly assisted by the power of connection through group therapy. In my view healing is not solely an individual process but a social one, and people benefit greatly from witnessing the shared humanity in others' struggles. Group therapy fosters emotional honesty and a sense of communal support.

## Mind-body awareness

There is ever-increasing awareness that mind and body are intrinsically connected, so healing requires attention to both psychological and physical symptoms. Trauma therapy uses practices such as mindfulness, body awareness and somatic therapy to help people reconnect with their bodies and address how trauma is stored physically. These practices encourage clients to listen to their bodies and understand how trauma shows up in physical ailments.

## Reframing trauma narratives

Narrative therapy techniques also help in healing from trauma. Participants reframe their personal stories: by exploring the narratives that shape their identities, particularly those shaped by trauma, they can shift their perceptions and heal. Trauma is seen not as a life-defining event but as something that can be integrated and overcome.

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is often associated with children, but it is increasingly recognised in adults as well. Many adults with ADHD have had the condition since childhood but were never diagnosed. For them the symptoms can affect work, relationships and overall wellbeing. Psychological treatments offer effective strategies for managing ADHD in adults and improving quality of life.

## The challenges of adult ADHD

Adult ADHD is characterised by persistent patterns of inattention, impulsivity and hyperactivity. Symptoms show up as difficulty focusing on tasks, disorganisation, restlessness and impulsive decision-making. Unlike children, whose hyperactivity is usually more visible, adults often experience internal restlessness and impulsivity, which makes it hard to maintain relationships, hold down a job or manage daily responsibilities.

## The role of psychological treatment

Medication is a common treatment for ADHD, but psychological therapies play a crucial role in addressing the behavioural and cognitive aspects of the disorder. They equip you with skills and strategies to manage your symptoms. The main psychological treatments for adult ADHD are cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), mindfulness-based therapies and coaching.

## 1. Cognitive behavioural therapy

CBT is one of the best-researched and most effective psychological treatments for adult ADHD. It focuses on changing the negative thought patterns and behaviours that contribute to difficulties. Through CBT, adults learn to:

- **Improve time management.** Breaking tasks into smaller steps, setting reminders and using planners.
- **Build organisational skills.** Decluttering, organising workspaces and developing routines that reduce chaos.
- **Develop problem-solving skills.** Approaching challenges systematically, which reduces impulsivity and leads to more considered decisions.
- **Manage stress and emotions.** Identifying triggers for stress and emotional dysregulation, and finding healthier ways to cope.

## 2. Mindfulness-based therapies

Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) and mindfulness meditation are gaining ground as complementary treatments for ADHD. They help you become more aware of your thoughts, feelings and bodily sensations in the present moment. For adults with ADHD, mindfulness can:

- **Reduce impulsivity**, by cultivating a non-judgemental awareness of the present moment so you can pause before acting on impulse.
- **Improve focus and attention**, by training attention to remain in the present.
- **Improve emotional regulation**, through a greater awareness of emotions that lets you respond to stress more calmly.

## 3. ADHD coaching

ADHD coaching is a specialised form of life coaching for the particular needs of people with ADHD. A coach works with you to set goals, develop action plans and create systems that support better management of symptoms. Coaching helps adults with ADHD to:

- **Reach personal and professional goals**, by setting realistic goals and breaking them into manageable steps.
- **Build accountability**, through regular check-ins that keep plans on track.
- **Improve self-esteem**, by focusing on strengths and successes.

## Bringing treatment into daily life

For psychological treatment to work, it has to be built into daily life. That takes commitment to regular practice and a willingness to make changes. Many people find that a combination of therapy, coaching and lifestyle changes, such as regular exercise, a balanced diet and adequate sleep, gives the best results.

Managing ADHD as an adult can be hard, but psychological treatment offers effective tools. By addressing the cognitive, behavioural and emotional aspects of ADHD, therapies like CBT, mindfulness-based approaches and coaching help adults overcome their difficulties and reach their goals.

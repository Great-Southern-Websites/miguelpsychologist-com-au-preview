Mental health is an integral part of our overall wellbeing, and protecting it is key to a good quality of life. No one is immune to mental health suffering, and the challenges are a lot harder for people with physical disabilities, because of limited mobility and difficulty performing basic tasks. Psychological support under the NDIS can significantly improve the quality of life of a person with a physical disability.

The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) is a government-funded initiative that offers psychological and financial support to people with physical and mental disabilities so they can lead independent and meaningful lives. A psychologist registered under the NDIS helps participants manage their mental health, cope with emotional challenges, and build resilience for the difficulties of life. Here is how.

## 1. Addressing mental health challenges

Living with a disability can bring particular emotional and psychological challenges, such as anxiety, depression or social isolation, which are hard to manage without professional support. Through the NDIS, participants can access therapy and counselling to deal better with their emotions and develop healthy coping mechanisms.

## 2. Developing positive behaviours

Some people with disabilities face behavioural challenges that affect their relationships and daily life. Psychologists working under the NDIS offer behaviour support plans that help participants develop positive behaviours and reduce harmful or disruptive ones. The plans are tailored to each participant's needs, which makes them highly effective. ## 3. Improving emotional resilience

One of the key benefits of psychological support is that it builds emotional resilience, so participants are better equipped to handle life's ups and downs. Through cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) and other therapies, NDIS participants learn to manage negative thoughts, overcome fears and focus on their strengths, which lets them face challenges with greater confidence and hope.

## 4. Building life skills for independence

Psychological support also helps people with disabilities build essential life skills, including problem-solving, decision-making and communication, all of which are vital for a more independent life. With these skills, participants can manage daily tasks and handle social situations more effectively.

## 5. Promoting long-term mental wellbeing

Psychological support under the NDIS is not only about short-term solutions. It aims to promote long-term wellbeing by teaching people how to care for their mental health throughout their lives, so they keep their emotional stability even as they face new challenges or changes in their condition.

Do you have an appointment with a psychologist coming up? Is it the first time you have approached a psychologist to deal better with your mental health? Starting therapy can be a big step towards improving your mental health, but the idea of a first session might feel a bit overwhelming. Preparing in advance can make the experience smoother and more beneficial. Here are some simple tips to help you get ready.

## 1. Understand what to expect

Not knowing what will happen in a session can cause anxiety. The first session is more like an icebreaker, where you and I get to know each other. I will ask about your background, your current issues and what you expect from therapy, and then explain my approach and how therapy equips you with tools to handle your issues better.

## 2. Reflect on your goals

Before your appointment, spend some time thinking about why you are seeking therapy. Are you dealing with stress, anxiety or relationship issues? A clear idea of your goals helps you communicate with your psychologist. It is fine if your goals change over time, but starting with a general sense of what you hope to achieve gives the work direction.

## 3. Write down your concerns

It is natural to feel nervous or unsure about what to discuss. Writing down your main concerns, symptoms or questions before the session helps you stay focused and makes sure you do not forget anything important. The list can include specific issues you are facing, feelings you have been experiencing, or questions about the therapy process.

## 4. Gather relevant information

If you have any medical or psychological history that might be relevant, such as previous diagnoses, treatments or medications, bring that information with you. It also helps to note any significant life events or changes that may be affecting your mental health. Sharing this background gives me a complete and clear picture of your situation. You can also [follow my work on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/miguelpsychologist/) to see how I help clients lead a better quality of life.

## 5. Be open and honest

Therapy works best when you are open and honest with your psychologist. I am trained to handle sensitive information and to provide support without judgement. Sharing your true feelings and experiences leads to a more meaningful and effective process.

## 6. Have realistic expectations

Therapy is a process that takes time. It is unlikely that all your issues will be resolved in the first session. Be patient with yourself and with the process. Building a strong therapeutic relationship and working through your concerns happens gradually.

The National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) provides essential support to Australians living with disability, but meeting participants' psychosocial needs remains a critical part of improving their quality of life. Psychosocial support helps people manage their mental health and deal with the complexities of living with a disability. This article looks at how better psychosocial treatment support benefits NDIS participants and how it can be improved.

## What psychosocial support is

Psychosocial support addresses a person's mental, emotional and social wellbeing. For NDIS participants, it means attending not only to their physical disability but also to the mental health challenges that come with it, so that their overall life experience improves.

## Why it matters

**Improved mental health.** Many people with disabilities face higher risks of depression and anxiety. Effective psychosocial support helps manage those conditions, leading to better mental health and daily functioning.

**Better quality of life.** Support that focuses on emotional wellbeing and social inclusion helps participants feel more connected and engaged.

**Better management of disability.** Coping strategies and emotional support leave participants better equipped to handle the challenges of their condition.

## Strategies for better support

**Integrated mental health services.** NDIS participants often have complex needs that call for a coordinated approach. Integrating mental health services with other supports, through collaboration between psychologists, counsellors and support workers, gives participants complete care.

**Personalised support plans.** Support plans should be tailored to each participant, working with them to identify their particular psychosocial needs and preferences and then setting strategies and goals.

**Access to counselling and therapy.** Individual and group therapy options need to be available, accessible and affordable.

**Promoting social inclusion.** Encouraging social engagement and community participation reduces isolation. Programs that arrange social activities and build connections with others make a real difference.

**Monitoring and evaluation.** Support services should be assessed regularly, gathering participants' feedback, evaluating outcomes and adjusting the support as needed.

Improving psychosocial support for NDIS participants means integrating mental health services, personalising support, training support workers and promoting social inclusion, so that every participant receives the care they need to do well.
